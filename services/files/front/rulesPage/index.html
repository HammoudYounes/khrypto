Introduction

Khet 2.0 is a strategic 1v1 board game played on a 10×10 grid. Each player commands an army of Egyptian-themed pieces with reflective mirrors, shields, and a deadly laser.

At the end of every turn, your Sphinx fires its laser. The beam bounces off mirrors, passes through empty cells, and destroys any piece it hits on a vulnerable face. Your ultimate goal: destroy the opponent's Pharaoh.

👥 2 Players
🏁 10×10 Grid
🔫 Laser Combat
🏆 Destroy the Pharaoh
♟️

The Pieces

Each piece occupies 1 cell on the grid. Learn their abilities to master the battlefield.

Sphinx

⚔️ Offensive

The main offensive piece. The Sphinx has a laser on one of its faces that fires automatically at the end of every turn.

  • 🛡️ Indestructible — Shields on all 4 sides
  • 🔫 Laser — 1 face has a laser that fires each turn
  • 🚫 Cannot move — Only rotates or swaps
  • 🔄 Rotatable — Can be rotated 90° to redirect the laser

Pharaoh

👑 King

The most important piece on the board. Losing your Pharaoh means losing the game.

  • 💀 4 vulnerable faces — No shields, no mirrors
  • 🚫 Cannot move — Only swaps with Scarab
  • 🚫 Cannot rotate — Orientation doesn't matter
  • ⚠️ Protect at all costs!

Anubis

🛡️ Protector

The protector. Position the shield face toward the enemy laser to block it. But beware — the other 3 faces are exposed.

  • 🛡️ 1 shield face — Blocks and stops the laser
  • 💀 3 vulnerable faces — Destroyed if hit
  • ↕️ Can move — 1 cell orthogonally (up/down/left/right)
  • 🔄 Rotatable — Rotate to redirect the shield

Pyramid

🪞 Mirror

The core piece. Features a diagonal mirror that reflects the laser 90°. Position and rotate carefully to build laser paths — or disrupt your opponent's.

  • 🪞 Diagonal mirror — Reflects laser on 2 faces
  • 💀 2 vulnerable faces — The non-mirror sides
  • ↕️ Can move — 1 cell orthogonally
  • 📦 Reserve — Each player has 7 extra Pyramids to place
A Pyramid reflecting the laser beam 90°

Scarab

✨ Special

A powerful piece with a double-sided diagonal mirror. It reflects lasers from both sides and is completely indestructible. It can also swap positions with your Sphinx or Pharaoh.

  • 🪞 Double mirror — Reflects laser from BOTH sides
  • 🛡️ Indestructible — Always redirects, never destroyed
  • ↕️ Can move — 1 cell orthogonally
  • 🔀 Swap — Can swap with Sphinx or Pharaoh (cooldown of 4 turns applies for each swap type)

Initial Setup

The board is set up randomly following specific placement rules. Player 2's pieces are centrally symmetrical to Player 1.

Player 1 Placement

  1. Sphinx — Placed on a random column of the 1st row. Laser faces horizontally toward the side with the most available cells.
  2. Pharaoh — Placed on a random cell on the 3rd row. Excluded: Columns 1, 10, own Sphinx column, opponent's Sphinx column.
  3. Anubis 1 — Placed on the 5th row, same column as the Pharaoh, facing the opponent.
  4. Anubis 2 — Placed on the 3rd row, in the opponent's Sphinx column, facing the opponent.
  5. Scarab — Random position on the 4th row, random orientation.
  6. Reserve — Each player receives a box outside the grid containing 7 Pyramids for later placement.

Player 2 — Symmetry

🔄

Player 2's pieces are placed with central symmetry (180° rotation of the board). This ensures a fair and balanced starting position.

💡

Because placement is randomized, every game starts with a unique board configuration!

How to Play

Players take turns. Each turn, you perform exactly 1 action, then your Sphinx fires automatically.

1

Rotate 90°

Sphinx · Anubis · Scarab · Pyramid

Rotate any of these pieces 90° clockwise or counter-clockwise. This changes which direction the piece's shield, mirror, or laser faces. Rotating is key to setting up laser paths and defending your Pharaoh.

2

Move (Orthogonal)

Anubis · Pyramid · Scarab

Move a piece 1 cell in any orthogonal direction (up, down, left, right). The target cell must be empty — you cannot move onto an occupied cell.

⚠️

Sphinx and Pharaoh cannot move — they stay fixed on the board.

Valid movement cells are highlighted in green
3

Place a Pyramid

From reserve → grid

Take a Pyramid from your reserve box and place it on any empty cell on the grid. You can freely choose its orientation when placing it.

🚫

Placement restriction: Cannot place a Pyramid orthogonally adjacent (up/down/left/right) to your own Pharaoh or any Sphinx.

The reserve box — drag a Pyramid onto the board
4

Swap (Scarab)

Scarab ↔ Sphinx or Pharaoh

Swap the Scarab's position with your own Sphinx or Pharaoh. This is a powerful defensive/offensive maneuver to reposition key pieces.

⏱️

Cooldown: Each swap target (Sphinx/Pharaoh) has a 4-turn cooldown after being swapped. You cannot swap with the same target until the cooldown expires.

💡

Special rule: A swapped Sphinx keeps its orientation but does NOT fire its laser on the turn it was swapped.

Swap targets are highlighted when selecting a Scarab
⚡

End of Turn — Laser Fires

Automatic · Every turn

After your action, your Sphinx fires its laser automatically. The laser beam travels in a straight line from the Sphinx's laser face.

🪞

Mirrors: The laser bounces 90° off Pyramid and Scarab mirror faces

💥

Destroys: Hits a non-reflective face → piece is destroyed, laser continues

🛡️

Shields: Hits a shield face (Anubis/Sphinx) → laser stops completely

🚫

Off-grid: Laser exits the board → it stops

💡

Cannonball style: The laser acts as a single particle. If it destroys a piece mid-path, the path does not recompute — it continues as if the piece was never there.

Additional Rules

🏆

Win Condition

Destroy your opponent's Pharaoh with your laser. The game ends immediately.

🤝

Draw

The game is a draw if both Pharaohs are destroyed on the same turn, or if no winner emerges after 100 turns.

💥

Destroying a Piece

A laser hitting a non-reflective side of a Pyramid or a non-shield face of an Anubis destroys the piece. The laser continues through as if the piece was never there.

🚧

Collision

You cannot move or place a piece onto an already occupied cell. The target cell must be empty.

♻️

Recycling

When a Pyramid is destroyed, it goes to the opponent's reserve box after a 1-turn delay. This means destroyed pyramids can come back to fight for the other side!

⏳

Turn Limit

The game has a maximum of 100 turns. If neither Pharaoh is destroyed by then, the game ends in a draw.
